## What changed

Edition 1.0 incorrectly limited the first AI delivery qualification to TypeScript/JavaScript web applications. Edition 1.1 separates the toolkit implementation from the AI engineering remit. It adds technology-agnostic requirements, task-specific specialist context, native/model/polyglot validation responsibilities, and broader qualification criteria without rewriting the toolkit in many languages.

The six additional requirements are traced through existing tasks. Fourteen scenario groups gained cross-stack assertions. The EngineeringContext schema accepts arbitrary technology identifiers and rejects tested malformed provenance/gap declarations. A syntactically valid context is not proof its source pointers are authentic or that its environment is available. Actual source resolution, task routing, native execution, permissions and final acceptance remain product implementation requirements.

The reference evaluator and database implementation files were not changed. Existing reference behavior was rerun rather than assumed to pass. Domain entities retain their existing versions; EngineeringContext is additive. The source-level ProviderContext interface gains a required engineering_context field, which downstream implementers must populate.

## Explicitly not performed

No authenticated Claude Code/Codex run, native plugin install, actual target-platform build, simulator/device interaction, LLM/model evaluation, full Client Mode implementation, protected-service isolation test, live benchmark, holdout qualification or production release was performed. Full OpenAPI tooling conformance is not claimed; its checks here are structural. No newly researched vendor claims were added.

Every product acceptance scenario remains `NOT_EXECUTED_PRODUCT_TEST`. G0 validates the handoff; G1–G6 still require engineers to build and execute the system. “All stacks” is a required engineering remit and adaptation behavior, not a guarantee of omniscience or universal production certification.
